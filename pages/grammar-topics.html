13 Core Domains

The Competitive English Grammar Syllabus

Arranged into three linguistic tiers. Each domain isolates the high-frequency trap examiners construct, the mathematical rule to counter it, and the companion Kindle handbook.

Tier 1: Syntactic & Concord Foundations

Weightage: 35–45% of Error Spotting
Core Subject Concord

Subject-Verb Agreement

The foundation of all sentence syntax. Governs singular and plural agreement across parenthetical phrases, fractions, compound nouns, and inverted questions.

The Examiner Trap: "The quality of these mangoes were was substandard."

Zero-Error Formula: When prepositional phrases separate subject and verb, the verb strictly agrees with the head noun (quality), never the intervening object (mangoes).

Temporal Sequence

Tenses & Time Anchors

Time anchors govern verb aspect. Past perfect vs simple past, continuous aspect restrictions with stative verbs, and future time conjunctions.

The Examiner Trap: "By the time the ambulance arrived, the patient died had died."

Zero-Error Formula: When two actions happened in the past, the earlier completed action requires Past Perfect (had + V3) and the later action takes Simple Past (V2).

Companion: Handbook 2 View Guide
Pronominal Concord

Noun & Pronoun Consistency

Case sensitivity (subjective vs objective pronoun), indefinite pronoun agreement (one must do one's duty), and antecedent gender/number matching.

The Examiner Trap: "One should always respect his one's elders in society."

Zero-Error Formula: When the indefinite pronoun "one" serves as the subject, all subsequent possessive and reflexive pronouns must strictly be one's and oneself.

Companion: Handbook 9 View Guide
Modal Expressions

Modal Auxiliaries & Semi-Modals

Nuances of obligation (must vs should), deduction in the past (must have + V3), ability, permission, and modal perfective constructions.

The Examiner Trap: "He should had attended should have attended the symposium."

Zero-Error Formula: Modal verbs are invariably followed by base form bare infinitive (have), never the inflected past form (had).

Tier 2: Relational, Modifier & Determiner Rules

Weightage: 30–35% of Error Spotting & Fillers
Spatial & Idiomatic

Fixed Prepositions & Phrasals

Verbs taking specific fixed prepositions, Latin comparative adjectives, verbs taking no prepositions, and phrasal verb confusion in Cloze Tests.

The Examiner Trap: "The board discussed about the merger for three hours."

Zero-Error Formula: Transitive verbs such as discuss, describe, order, enter, resemble take a direct object with zero intervening prepositions.

Companion: Handbook 3 View Guide
Specificity & Reference

Articles & Determiners

Definite article "the" with geographical features, institutions (school/hospital for primary purpose), and omission with uncountable abstract nouns.

The Examiner Trap: "He was sent to the prison prison for committing the theft."

Zero-Error Formula: Nouns like school, church, prison, hospital omit "the" when visited for their primary constitutive purpose.

Companion: Handbook 7 View Guide
Gradation & Qualification

Adjectives & Adverbs

Comparative doublets, absolute adjectives that cannot be graded (unique, dead, perfect), and the position of "enough" relative to adjectives.

The Examiner Trap: "This artefact is the most unique unique piece in the collection."

Zero-Error Formula: Absolute adjectives (unique, complete, universal, circular) indicate a maximum state and cannot be qualified by "more" or "most".

Companion: Handbook 11 View Guide
Correlative Syntax

Conjunctions & Inversion

Correlative pairs (neither...nor, not only...but also), negative adverb inversion (hardly had he...when), and lest + should constraints.

The Examiner Trap: "Work diligently lest you will fail should fail the examination."

Zero-Error Formula: The conjunction "lest" expresses negative intention and strictly pairs with the auxiliary "should" (or subjunctive base verb).

Tier 3: Structural Transformations & Complex Syntax

Weightage: 20–25% of Mains Tier II / Sentence Correction
Voice Transformation

Active & Passive Voice

Passive of imperative sentences (let + be + V3), verbs with two objects, quasi-passive verbs, and stative verbs governing prepositions other than "by".

The Examiner Trap: "His performance satisfied the panel. → The panel was satisfied by was satisfied with his performance."

Zero-Error Formula: Stative verbs govern fixed prepositions in passive: satisfied with, pleased with, known to, astonished at.

Companion: Handbook 5 View Guide
Reported Speech

Direct & Indirect Speech

Backshifting rules, exceptions for universal truths, reporting wh-questions (avoiding inverted word order), and imperative reporting with to-infinitives.

The Examiner Trap: "He asked me where was I living I was living."

Zero-Error Formula: In indirect reported questions, interrogative inversion is replaced with assertive word order: Wh-word + Subject + Verb.

Companion: Handbook 6 View Guide
Hypothetical Mood

Conditionals & Subjunctive Mood

Zero, First, Second, and Third Conditionals. Subjunctive mood expressing wishes, demands, and unreal hypothetical pasts.

The Examiner Trap: "I wish I was were present at the conference."

Zero-Error Formula: Unreal hypothetical wishes and contrary-to-fact conditions require subjunctive "were" for all singular and plural persons.

Companion: Handbook 8 View Guide
Syntactic Symmetry

Parallelism & Modifiers

Coordinated phrases must share identical syntactic structure (gerund with gerund, infinitive with infinitive). Dangling participles with unstated subjects.

The Examiner Trap: "Walking down the road, a brick hit him he was hit by a brick."

Zero-Error Formula: A participial phrase must be followed immediately by the noun that performed the action (the brick did not walk down the road).
