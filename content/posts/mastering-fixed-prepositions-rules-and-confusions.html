Unlike vocabulary or basic tenses where logic can guide you, Fixed Prepositions are idiomatic collocations governed by established convention. In competitive English, they are the number one source of negative marking.

1. "Differ with" vs "Differ from"

This pair causes immense confusion. Here is the definitive distinction:

  • Differ with: Used when disagreeing with a person in opinion or perspective.
  • Differ from: Used when indicating contrast in physical appearance, nature, or quality between things or people.
✕ Common Error
I differ from you on this political subject.
When disagreeing with a person's opinion, use "differ with".
✓ Correct Sentence
I differ with you on this political subject.
"Differ with a person" on an issue.

2. "Die of" vs "Die from"

Examiners frequently test this medical distinction in cloze tests and error spotting:

The Cause of Death Principle

Use die of when the cause is an internal disease, hunger, thirst, or shame (e.g., He died of malaria). Use die from when the cause is an indirect or external factor like loss of blood, overwork, or food poisoning (e.g., He died from over-exhaustion).

3. Verbs that Take NO Preposition

Beware of transitive verbs that examiners incorrectly attach prepositions to:

  • Do NOT say: discuss about (Say: discuss the matter)
  • Do NOT say: enter into the room (Say: enter the room)
  • Do NOT say: resemble to his father (Say: resembles his father)
  • Do NOT say: order for coffee (Say: order coffee)
