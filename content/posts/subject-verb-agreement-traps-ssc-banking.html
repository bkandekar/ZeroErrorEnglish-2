In competitive examinations like SSC CGL, CHSL, and IBPS PO, Subject-Verb Agreement questions look deceptively straightforward. Yet, statistical analysis reveals that over 65% of test-takers lose marks on this very topic.

The reason? Question setters know how our human eyes process language. They plant intervening words, inverted clauses, and distracting plural nouns to trick you into matching the verb with the nearest word instead of the true subject.

Fundamental Principle
The Proximity Fallacy Rule

A verb must agree with its grammatical subject in person and number. The proximity of an intervening noun—regardless of whether it is plural or singular—does not alter the subject's number.

Trap #1: The Parenthetical Phrase Disguise

When phrases such as along with, as well as, together with, in addition to, accompanied by, besides, and like join a secondary noun to the subject, they are parenthetical. The verb agrees strictly with the first subject.

✕ Exam Trap (Incorrect)
The Prime Minister, along with his cabinet ministers, are visiting the flood-affected districts.
"Are" is wrong because "along with his cabinet ministers" is parenthetical. The subject is the singular "Prime Minister".
✓ Correct Sentence
The Prime Minister, along with his cabinet ministers, is visiting the flood-affected districts.
Singular subject "Prime Minister" matches singular verb "is".

Trap #2: Either / Neither as Singular Pronouns

When used alone as subjects or when followed by "of the + plural noun", Either and Neither are strictly singular and require a singular verb.

Examiner's Favorite Trap

Examiners love writing: "Neither of the two candidates have submitted...". The plural noun "candidates" distracts you, but "Neither" is the subject and strictly commands has!

Interactive Practice Drill

Exam Practice Check
A bouquet of freshly cut yellow roses ______ delivered to the professor's office this morning.
Memory Anchor

Mentally bracket any phrase beginning with a preposition (of, in, with, on, by) between the subject and the verb. Read the sentence skipping the bracketed part to immediately detect number mismatch!
