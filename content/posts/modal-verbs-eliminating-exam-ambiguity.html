Modal auxiliaries convey the speaker's attitude: necessity, obligation, probability, permission, or past regret. Misidentifying the intended tone leads directly to error.

Obligation Scale: Should vs Ought to vs Must

  • Should: Expresses general advice, recommendation, or suggestion (You should sleep early).
  • Ought to: Expresses moral duty, social obligation, or strong ethical expectation (We ought to respect our national flag).
  • Must: Expresses compulsory legal or administrative obligation, or logical certainty (You must carry your original ID to the examination center).
Exam Trap: Past Regrets with "Should Have"

Candidates frequently confuse should have (something didn't happen, but was desirable) with must have (strong past logical deduction). For example: "He must have reached home by now" (deduction) vs "He should have submitted the form yesterday" (failure to do so).
